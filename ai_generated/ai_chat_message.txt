Step 2 is done — a tested, dependency-free core you can build the rest of the project on top of.
What's in it: the pure BKT math (posterior, update, soft-evidence for partial credit, entropy, expected information gain), the ConceptGraph (DAG with cycle detection, topological order, ancestor/descendant closure), and the KnowledgeTracer that ties them together with the prerequisite-gated priors, frontier-based selection, leverage-based recommendation, and gap report. Twenty tests pass, and the final one runs a synthetic learner with a known ground-truth mastery vector through the full loop and checks the inferred gaps match — that's your eval-on-yourself story working with zero real users.
One thing worth flagging from the build: the first version had a real modeling bug where one unlucky slip could permanently write a concept off and starve everything downstream of it. The min_questions floor fixes it, and I verified across 30 seeds (mean 98% accuracy, worst case 78%) rather than tuning to one lucky run. That bug-and-fix is itself a good interview anecdote — it shows you understand the failure modes of the model, not just the happy path.
A couple of honest caveats so you build on solid ground. The BKT parameters are global constants right now; in a real system you'd fit per-concept slip/guess from data (or at least vary them by difficulty), and being able to say "here's how I'd calibrate these" is a good thing to have a plan for. And the partial-credit update is a pragmatic interpolation, not strict Bayes — it's documented as such in the code, which is the right way to handle it.
